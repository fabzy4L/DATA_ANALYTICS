Numerical Comparison: Computational Results vs. Experimental Literature

Analysis by Fabian A. Alvarez-Primo, Ph.D. | May 1, 2026

This report documents a direct comparison between the experimental binding values reported in established peer-reviewed literature and the computational results generated through molecular docking simulations in this project.

1. Experimental Literature Values (Inhibition Constant, Ki)

In experimental pharmacology, the Inhibition Constant (Ki) measures the concentration required to occupy 50% of the receptor population. Lower values indicate higher binding affinity.

Source Δ9-THC (Ki) Δ8-THC (Ki) Observation
Tagen et al. (2022) ~25 nM ~165 nM Δ9 binds tighter
Hua et al. (2016) 40.7 ± 1.7 nM 47.6 ± 1.9 nM Δ9 binds tighter

Literature Consensus: Experimental studies consistently demonstrate that Δ9-THC exhibits a higher binding affinity for the CB1 receptor than its Δ8 isomer in biological systems.

2. Project Computational Results (Gibbs Free Energy, ΔG)

In computational docking, the scoring function provides an estimate of the Gibbs Free Energy (ΔG) of binding. More negative values indicate higher predicted binding affinity.

Ligand Isomer Predicted Affinity (ΔG) Result Classification
Δ8-Tetrahydrocannabinol -6.5 kcal/mol Predicted Tighter Binder
Δ9-Tetrahydrocannabinol -6.3 kcal/mol Predicted Weaker Binder

Project Consensus: The in silico simulation predicts that Δ8-THC has a marginally higher binding affinity than Δ9-THC under static conditions.

3. Comparative Analysis: The "Inversion of Preference"

A notable divergence exists between the computational predictions and the experimental data. While the literature favors Δ9-THC, the AutoDock Vina algorithm used in this project favors Δ8-THC by a margin of 0.2 kcal/mol.

Scientific Rationale:

  • Static vs. Dynamic Environments: The docking simulation utilized a "frozen" crystal structure (PDB: 5TGZ). In this state, the Δ8 configuration optimizes van der Waals contacts that are prominent in a static model.
  • Entropic Factors: Biological Ki values account for receptor flexibility and desolvation, which often favor Δ9-THC in ways static docking cannot.
  • Induced Fit: Δ9-THC likely induces a conformational change in the live receptor that maximizes binding—a dynamic effect missing in rigid-receptor docking.

Conclusion

Dr. Alvarez-Primo's results successfully identify both molecules as thermodynamically favorable binders. The discrepancy between computational preference and experimental data serves as a vital case study in the limitations of in silico screening and emphasizes the need for future Molecular Dynamics (MD) validation.

Comparative Structural and Computational Analysis of the S438T Mutation in the Human Serotonin Transporter

Fabian A. Alvarez-Primo, Ph.D.

Computational Structural Biology | May 1, 2026

Abstract: The human Serotonin Transporter (SERT, SLC6A4) is the primary molecular target for Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs). This study investigates the S438T mutation, a critical determinant of SSRI sensitivity in the S1 pocket. We document a corrective pivot from an initial investigation of the A348T site (a digit-swap error). Our results show that escitalopram binds to the S438T mutant with high affinity (−8.3 kcal/mol), with a minimal ΔΔG of −0.2 kcal/mol relative to wild-type, suggesting structural resilience in the rigid-receptor model.

1. Introduction

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) prevent the reuptake of serotonin by binding to the orthosteric site (S1) of SERT. Serine 438 is a key residue in this pocket. The S438T mutation introduces a methyl group that has been shown to sterically hinder ligands like citalopram.

This report rectifies a prior error where A348T was analyzed instead of S438T. Position 348 is a peripheral site in TM6, while 438 is a direct ligand-contact residue. We present here the definitive results for the correct site.

2. Methods

Receptors were prepared from PDB 5I6Z. The S438T mutation was introduced via the NERF algorithm with the favored g+ rotamer (χ1 = +60°). Escitalopram was docked using AutoDock Vina 1.2.5 with a grid box centered on the S1 pocket and an exhaustiveness of 8.

3. Results

A structural audit confirmed that residue 348 is a peripheral Alanine, while residue 438 is the functional pocket Serine. The results for the corrected site are shown below:

Wild-Type SER −8.1
Mutant THR −8.3

The mutation resulted in a slight affinity increase (ΔΔG = −0.2 kcal/mol), indicating that escitalopram can accommodate the Threonine side chain within the S1 cavity under rigid-receptor conditions.

4. Discussion

The small ΔΔG shift suggests that escitalopram (the S-enantiomer) may be less sensitive to the S438T "steric clash" than citalopram. The additional methyl group of Threonine 438 appears to be housed without displacing primary pharmacophoric contacts. Future molecular dynamics studies will explore potential kinetic impacts.

5. Conclusion

Correcting the site of study from 348 to 438 provides the first valid computational assessment of this mutation in the project. Escitalopram retains high affinity for the S438T mutant, providing a robust baseline for further pharmaceutical research.
